# August 3, 2026 - Batch LAND withdrawal from an Estate was authorized once for the whole batch

|                          |                |
| -----------------------: | :------------- |
|             **Reported** | August-03-2026 |
|           **Mitigation** | August-04-2026 |
|   **Solution Completed** | August-05-2026 |

## Description

- `EstateRegistry` custodies the LAND parcels composed into an Estate and lets the Estate owner, or
  an operator they approved, withdraw them. Two entry points do it: `transferLand` for one parcel
  and `transferManyLands` for a list. Both carried the same `canTransfer(estateId)` modifier, but
  the batch one evaluated it **once**, before the loop, and then performed one withdrawal per
  parcel.
- Every withdrawal ends in `registry.safeTransferFrom(this, destinatary, landId)`, and
  `destinatary` is chosen freely by the caller. When it is a contract, `LANDRegistry` calls
  `onERC721Received` on it, so the caller gets a call frame with the remaining iterations still
  pending. The account debited is re-read inside the loop (`owner = ownerOf(estateId)`), so it
  follows whoever owns the Estate at that instant.
- The batch therefore moves a different total than the same calls made one at a time. Three
  separate `transferLand` calls with a sale in between move one parcel — the second and third
  revert because the caller is no longer authorized. One `transferManyLands` over the same three
  parcels moves all three.
- The realized impact is theft from the buyer of the Estate. The attacker starts the batch and,
  inside the first transfer callback, sells the Estate into a standing bid through `ERC721Bid` — a
  path where the owner pushes the Estate in, so it composes cleanly inside the callback. The sale
  settles, the attacker takes the MANA, and the remaining iterations keep withdrawing parcels out
  of the Estate that now belongs to the buyer. A 100-parcel Estate leaves the buyer with one.
- The protection that exists for selling composable NFTs did not stop it. `ERC721Bid` verifies the
  Estate fingerprint the bidder signed, and the fingerprint was an order-independent XOR over the
  parcel list. Re-inserting the first parcel inside the callback left the list in a different order
  with a bit-identical fingerprint, so the check passed on a composition the bidder never agreed to.
- No privilege beyond the one the check is meant to grant was needed, and nothing else in the
  contract stopped it: there is no reentrancy guard, the per-parcel index check still passes for
  parcels legitimately still inside, and `LANDRegistry`'s own authorization sees the EstateRegistry
  as the holder on every iteration, regardless of who owns the Estate.

### Root cause

The authorization decision and the actions it authorized were separated by attacker-controlled code.
The modifier ran once on the external entry point, and then N withdrawals executed, each handing
control to an address the caller picked and each charging whoever owned the Estate at that moment. A
check placed on a batch entry point only holds for the first item unless the loop re-evaluates it.

## Severity

Smart Contracts - Medium.

The loss is direct and falls on a third party who did nothing wrong: the buyer pays for a
composition and receives a fraction of it. It is bounded per Estate and needs the attacker to own
the Estate and find a standing bid to sell into, which keeps it below High. It sat in the middle of
the tier rather than at the floor because the defective check was not peripheral — it governed how
parcels leave every Estate, in a contract holding roughly 70% of all LAND supply at the time.

## Solution

[Move the authorization check into the internal `_transferLand`](https://github.com/decentraland/land/pull/170)
(merged August 4, shipped as a new `EstateRegistry` implementation):

- **Re-check on every parcel (root cause).** `canTransfer(estateId)` was removed from both external
  entry points and applied to `_transferLand` itself, so it is evaluated once per parcel moved. A
  batch now reverts on the first iteration after the caller stops being authorized, and
  `transferManyLands` can no longer move more than the same calls made one at a time.
- **Reject transferring an empty Estate.** `transferFrom` now requires the Estate to hold at least
  one parcel, and reuses that value for the balance update instead of reading it twice.
- Confirmed no longer exploitable against a fork of the upgraded contract on August 5.
